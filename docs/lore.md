# Clockwork Spire: lore

The story is told in small pieces: event lines, the inventor's notes, the journal, the bestiary, residents' remarks, the wardens' phase lines, and the ending. Never a wall of text: one to three short sentences at a time. Voice: warm, curious, a little melancholy (v1). The inventor is never named in the game; the apprentice (the player) has no name either.

## The premise
Bellfoot is a small town at the foot of the Spire, a clock tower so tall its top is lost in cloud. The inventor built the Spire to keep the town's time: its bells told Bellfoot when to wake, work, eat and sleep. To tend it, the inventor built the Clockmaker, the finest machine of all, who loved the inventor the way a clock loves its key.

One evening the inventor climbed the Spire and did not come back down. The Clockmaker, who could not bear an hour without the inventor in it, stopped the hour. Since then it has been dusk in Bellfoot: the lamps never need lighting and never go out, bread never quite finishes rising, nobody ages, and nobody quite remembers how long it has been. Without the Clockmaker's care, the Spire's machines went feral: the gears grew teeth, the boilers grew tempers.

You are the inventor's apprentice. Sprocket was the inventor's dog; he waited at the Spire gate every evening until you came along, and now he climbs with you. You climb to find out what happened, and to start the hour again.

## The Spire
- **The Gearworks** (act 1): the tower's lowest section, the great gear trains that turn the hands. Warden: **the Foreman**, who kept the gear crews in line and still does, though the crews are rats and beetles now. He thinks the inventor is just late.
- **The Steamworks** (act 2): the boilers that wind the mainsprings. Warden: **the Boilermaker Queen**, built to keep the pressure even, who decided she was in charge of everything above the waterline. She is proud and lonely and runs too hot.
- **The Belfry** (act 3): the bells, the clock face and the escapement at the very top. Warden: **the Clockmaker**.

## The wardens' lines (phase beats)
Short; one per phase change and one on defeat. Drafts, final text in content:
- Foreman: "Shift's not over." / "Fine. FINE. Overtime." / defeat: "Tell the inventor... the gears are clean."
- Queen: "Mind the pressure, little one." / "You've cracked my gauge. Now I'll never know how hot I am." / defeat: "It's cold. I'd forgotten cold."
- Clockmaker: Tick: "You're early." / Tock: "I have all the time there is. I kept it." / Midnight: "If the hour ends, the inventor ends with it." / defeat: "...Then let it be morning."

## Bestiary voice (examples)
- Cog Rat: "Made to carry oil to the gear trains. Now it carries grudges."
- Rust Mite: "Ate the rust off the gears. Then the gears."
- Brass Beetle: "A polishing machine. Still polishes. Mostly you."
- Steam Wraith: "Not a ghost. Just steam that forgot to leave."
- Gearhound: "The Foreman's watchdog. Sprocket does not approve."
One line per enemy, written with the content catalog.

## Residents (who moves to Bellfoot, and what they say)
- **The Lamplighter**: has nothing to do, since the lamps never go out. Moves down when you fix the lift he's stuck on. "I'll light them anyway. Habit."
- **The Apprentice's Bench** event's young tinkerer: "The inventor let me hold the screwdriver once."
- **The Oil Merchant**: sells oil to a town where nothing wears out. Business is slow.
- **The Hour Ghost**: not a ghost; the old night watchman, faded from standing in one evening too long. Becomes the archivist's helper and tells the bestiary.
- **The Trader's cousin**: trades in the Spire because nobody in town needs anything.

## Rules for writing lore
- Every line is optional to read: nothing needed to play is only in lore.
- No villains: every machine is doing its old job wrongly. The wardens get one sympathetic line each.
- Sprocket never speaks; he reacts.
- Short. If a line runs past two sentences, cut it.
