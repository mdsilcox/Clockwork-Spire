## Cross-asset style (owner's main question)
Better but still two dialects. Sprocket and the title are flat cel fills with clear ink; the Boilermaker and Cog Rat now have thicker dark outlines at the silhouette and flatter fills than round 1, but are far denser (hundreds of small rendered brass details, gradient highlights on the glass, scepter orb and clock face) so they read as "detailed comic-book illustration" next to Sprocket's "simple cartoon". Same palette, same light direction, same brass/teal vocabulary, so they sit together in a scene, and the rat reads clearly as a rat now. Effects (soft blurry steam, glow bokeh, ghosted white hurt frame) are airbrushed, the one remaining element that contradicts the D-030 "no airbrush" rule. Acceptable for the owner to judge, but flag it as the open style question: is Sprocket the one to bring up in detail, or the enemies simplified?

## 2. Boilermaker Queen: style 3, rig 3.5, readability 4. REVISE (minor)

- Phase 15 shows a pale teal haze overlay over the whole body at the charge; slightly washed out.
- Idle breathing still very small.

## Commercial-indie bar
Sprocket and the title pass. The boss reads as a strong, characterful design, and the rat is good; both need hurt-state and effect polish to reach the bar for shipped enemies.

## Ranked improvements
1. Fix hurt frame 0 and add real recoil for the boss and rat (the only remaining must-fix).
2. Convert the soft white steam, bokeh and ghost-flash effects into ink-edged flat cel shapes so effects match D-030.
3. Add impact weight (squash, hold) to the Boilermaker slam and deepen the rat's crouch.
