## Blockers

1. **The Plating answer is not credible as written for regulars and act 3, and BV3/rules 7.4(3) cannot be satisfied.** Evidence is in section A. Until the enemy roster is checked against the spike's numbers (peak Plating 37 / 50 / 59 by act, up to 109 at p90), the owner's central complaint is answered by labels.
2. **Rules 7.4(3) and BV3 contain an impossible clause.** "The expert's win rate beats each of them by at least 15 points at the same bins" cannot hold on any bin where the turtle wins 86% or more. The spike has the turtle at 88 to 98% on every normal and boss tier except act 1 and act 2 elites (77%, 73%). Rule 7.4(3) also applies this to normal fights as a group, where all bots win 89 to 98%. A build team cannot make this green without changing the meaning. Define it on HP lost (a ratio) or restrict it to bins where the turtle wins under 80%.
3. **The trinket half of the rarity request is unanswered.** Playtest: "More tiers of rarity for game-changing parts and trinkets." Content section 5 keeps trinkets at Common, Uncommon, Rare and Boss (36 trinkets, 3 Rare, none Masterwork or Legendary). Either add Masterwork and Legendary trinkets (even 3 to 4, from vaults and wardens) or record the owner's decision to leave them out in DECISIONS.md.

## A. Does the design break "stack Plating, then burst"? (the central question)

What is good: sealed cores, keystones, no overkill carry, Pierce, Bulwark, Governor and Rewind are the right family of tools. The spike's key fact (Plating covering the shown incoming wins; pure burst wins 0%) is correctly identified, and the doc says the enemy must punish Plating. Damage lost to overkill and the target order give the player a real aiming game against elites and wardens.

What does not hold up:

- **Most of the "8 of 15 regulars punish Plating" tally (content 3.7) is not a Plating counter.** Real bypasses of Plating in the regulars: Pipe Snake Pierce 4x3, Furnace Golem Countdown Pierce 22, Bell Ringer Clapper Pierce 14, plus Steam Wraith's Siphon (heals the core by the Plating eaten, but Plating still absorbs the hit). That is 3 of 15, with 1 more weak. The other four tags are:
  - Cog Rat and Spring Imp (Ratchet): the Ratchet only grows if the core took no damage that turn, and the turtle and expert both attack the core every turn, so it never grows. It punishes a pure-defense stack that does no damage, which no bot or player does.
  - Oil Slick (Corrode 6): against mean peak Plating of 37 it removes 16% of an act 1 stack and about 10% of act 2 and act 3, a rounding error.
  - Pendulum Blade: attack escalates 8 to 24 and resets, all absorbed by Plating 59 mean. Its Counterweight is Ratchet again.
  EA11 only requires the tag in the data ("at least half punish Plating"), so the test passes on labels.
- **Act 1 has no Plating bypass at all among regulars.** Spike: turtle loses 7.3% of max HP in act 1 normals (3.7 HP over 2.9 turns). Enemy numbers are kept near v1 (content header), so v2 act 1 normals will read the same, and target 3 (turtle at least 10% on each act's normals) will fail with no lever named. Add a Pierce or Corrode part with real numbers to act 1 regulars, or raise act 1 damage.
- **Act 3 elites and the Clockmaker are Attack-based.** Orrery Arm Attack 24, Minute Hand 28, Midnight Bell 32/36, Crown 22 and Cinder Hand 22 are all absorbed by a 59-mean, 109-p90 stack. The real pressure there is Rewind, Jam, Rust and Drain (pressure on the machine, not HP). That is a good design instinct, but then HP lost is the wrong test metric (see improvement 2).
- **For regulars the cheapest line is still the core race.** A regular's core (Rust Mite 8, Cog Rat 8, Imp 10, Beetle 12, Slick 14) is smaller than its parts (Rat 18, Mite 10). Killing the core ends the enemy and wrecks all parts, so every punishment (Ratchet, Countdown 3, Corrode, heat) is irrelevant if the fight ends in about 3 turns (spike: normals last 2.6 to 3.4 turns). The Furnace Golem's Countdown 3 will never fire against a build that kills a core of 28 in 3 turns. "Break the part" is rewarded in salvage (a real, good tension: growth against speed), not forced. That is a defensible design, but pillar 5 ("the best answer is usually to break the right part") and 3.7's "punishes" contract overstate it for regulars. Say so, and let the sim check that salvage-greedy play costs HP or time.
- **Healing and debuffs.** The new Patch parts (Mending Spool 3 once a turn, Soothing Valve, Kettle 3) are small against 50 to 70 max HP and weaker than Plating, so nobody will pick them unless Pierce is common. Debuffs on the player (Rust, Jam, Corroded, Drain) are good. Healing's role depends on Pierce share, which is a tuning lever the docs never state.
- **The spike is v1 only.** No bot has been run against the v2 rules, so "the enemy parts break the solve" is unmeasured. A cheap, honest step before build: add a `damage that bypasses Plating per fight` metric to the sim spec and set a floor per act (for example at least 25% of an act's enemy damage potential), instead of discovering it in B-phase retunes.

## B. Wardens: are the phases guaranteed to be seen?

- **Seen: yes, structurally.** A sealed core cannot be hit until the keystones fall, the phase beat plays on the last keystone, WP1 forbids carry, so the Foreman (2 phases), Queen (3) and Clockmaker (3) cannot be skipped. That answers "I killed the final boss in 4 turns and didn't see his phases" better than v1 did.
- **Experienced for at least 2 turns each: no, only by the expert bot's statistics.** The owner's m-burst achievement is 100 damage in a turn. Clockmaker phase 1 keystones are 28 + 26 = 54 HP, phase 2 are 34 + 32 = 66, and phase 3's core is 84 with Governor cap 14 (six Strikes, or break the 24 HP Governor Frame). A strong human can clear each phase in one turn: about 3 to 4 turns total, which is the owner's 4. The spike said bots under-measure the owner's burst by about 2x and that v2 kill-speed targets "should come from the owner's line", but BV4 is again the expert bot. Make the 2-turn floor a rule (for example, every phase's first turn after the beat the new parts are shielded or the keystones arrive after a Shell equal to a strong turn), not a bot statistic, and add a "max burst" bot or a scripted 100-per-turn player to BV4.
- **Undefined mid-turn behavior (must pin down):** when the last keystone breaks on tick 1 or 2, do ticks 2 or 3 of the same Run hit the next phase's parts? Rules 4.8 cancels intents and says "no damage carries over", which reads as per-Strike overkill; WP1 only tests overkill within a Strike. If later ticks do hit the new phase, a 3-tick run can clear two phases in one turn.
- **Phase beat and action belong to which phase?** Content lists "Phase beat / Phase action" under each phase (Clockmaker phase 1: "Tick" and Rewind at once), while rules 4.8 says they play when the last keystone breaks. For the Clockmaker, Rewind "at once" on phase 1's line reads as playing at fight start (nothing on the board to lift). The data model `phases[i].{beat, action}` is equally ambiguous. State "plays on exit from phase i".
- **Cadence across phases.** Foreman's Rivet Gun is "3 of 3" in phase 2; if cadence counts combat turns, phase 2 (starting about turn 4) shows no Rivet until turn 6. Say whether cadence resets at a phase change.
- The doc targets disagree on length: Foreman "7 turns" (content, 3 plus 4) vs "8 turns" (vision walkthrough) vs 6 to 9 (BV4). Minor.

## C. Is the out-of-battle loop different from Slay the Spire?

Partly. Genuinely different: salvage instead of pick-1-of-3 (you take what you break), a clock that makes route choice a budget, visible deterministic elite patrols, fuse and barter, revisitable rooms, and residents and landmarks that persist between runs (the Spire remembers, the Clockmaker's memory). That is not a reskin.

Where it is still StS: the room taxonomy is the same (fights, elites, rest, shop, event, smith, boss), only the graph is now free-roam.

Weak points:
- **No loss state for the clock.** If midnight arrives, the warden simply comes where you stand. Nothing is lost for not reaching the door; the bell pays 4 Scrap and 2 Brass an hour while a fight pays 3 to 6 Scrap plus salvage plus 2 to 4 Brass for the same hour. The rational play is to spend every hour fighting and trading and never ring early. The door and the bell are dominated except as an achievement chase. Either let midnight cost something (for example fight the warden starting with less HP or hold fewer trader/oil rooms), or accept the clock as a pure budget and drop "against the clock" language.
- **Elites on a visible, deterministic patrol are a puzzle, not a threat.** You can always route around them or engineer the encounter. Fine, but call it that.
- **Hours against map geometry are unvalidated.** 5 to 6 floors means 4 to 5 mandatory moves to the door. At Journeyman (12) you have about 7 spare moves, at Clockwork with Overwind 2 (10 minus 1) you have about 4: roughly 4 fights an act, and a run's bin growth and Brass collapse relative to v1. CL1 should assert the shortest entry-to-door path (and hours minus that), not just room counts.
- **Undefined:** do elites step when you rest at an oil station or pick a lock (extra hours), or only after moves? What happens at midnight when you arrive in a trader or workbench room (can you use it)? The Overwind 5 "every 3rd hour" twist is keyed to hours, the base rule to moves.
- **Route extremes are not simulated.** The strategy spike covers turtle and burst only. Add a "rusher" (door by hour 6, ring the bell) and a "grinder" (fight everything) route bot so the curve cannot be dominated by either.

## D. Consistency and numbers

Contradictions or stale items found:
1. **Legendary source.** Content section 1 table: "The Boilermaker Queen's core only". Rules 4.4 and content Rule question 5: also the act 3 vault if you hold none. Vision: "from a boss's core in acts 2 and 3" (the Clockmaker drops nothing). Pick one. Note also that a Legendary first appears after act 2, so it shapes only act 3 plus nothing else.
2. **Overwind unlock.** Rules 5.7 and AD5: after a win on Journeyman or harder. Achievement `e-first-win`: win "on any mode" opens Overwind 1 to 3, so an Apprentice win opens it. The section 7 intro says conditions count on Journeyman or harder unless stated.
3. **Order of intents.** Rules 2.2: left to right on the frame. Rules 2.4: Corrode acts "before the enemy's attacks this turn". The Oil Slick works either way, but say which wins.
4. **Countdown and heat.** Rules 2.4 defines Countdown as ticking down by 1 (or a listed amount) and firing at 0. The Queen's Chest Gauge "Countdown 20" rises by 6 plus Pressure drained and fires at 20 (count up). The first tick (revealed on turn 1, fires on turn N) is not defined. Golem Heart: "Countdown 3" gives the player 3 builds or 2? Name it.
5. **Data model cannot express the content.** `cadence` has `number[] | 'every' | 'odd' | 'even' | {countdown}`; content uses "1 of 3", "2 of 3", "1,2 of 3", "1,3 of 3", "once", "passive" and per-phase cadence. `EnemyPartDef.action` is one `ActionDef`; the Midnight Bell has two (Attack 32 on odd, Jam and Attack 36 on even). `ActionDef.kind` has no heat, echo, purge, enrage or escalate (Spring Imp "+3 each time it acts", Blade "+4, resets after 24"); "Rewind 1 combination and Pressure to 0" (Tock Weight) has one amount. Update the data model with the shapes the content needs before build.
6. **Stale walkthrough in data-model.md**: Cog Rat {core 18, jaw 8, plate 10, shell 6} vs content (core 8, plate 6, tail 4).
7. **Archivist before the Hour Ghost.** The archivist is a Bellfoot place from the start (journal, bestiary, the Clockmaker's note, rules 5.1), but the Hour Ghost event says he "moves into the archivist's chair" and rules 5.4 gives the lore and bestiary to him. Who staffs it before that? WP6 and BF3 need an answer.
8. **Lore vs content phase lines.** `lore.md` drafts ("Shift's not over.", "Mind the pressure, little one.") differ from content's final lines ("Mind your fingers...", "Stay a while..."). Mark one as the source.
9. **Cogs vs Brass vs Brass Key.** Currency Brass and the item Brass Key share a word; consider "Iron Key" to avoid ambiguity in tooltips.
10. `acceptance.md` BV3 omits the "each act's normal fights as a group" part of rules 7.4(3), so the normal-fight claim is never tested.
11. EA11's thresholds (half punish Plating, a third punish burst) are tag counts, not behavior (see A).

Numbers that do work (checked): Foreman totals 204, Queen totals 306, Clockmaker 282/306, twin frames 65 each, early-bell arithmetic (CL5: 4 hours left gives 16 Scrap and 8 Brass), trinket counts 36, part counts 70, enemy counts (15 + 6 + 3), warden part counts within 8, EA6 floor-half, SV4 barter 40 and buy-alone 75. The Queen's heat is defusable in time: with 8 Pressure drained it fires on enemy turn 2 (6, then 6 + 8 + more), leaving two builds to break a 24 HP gauge. The Furnace Golem's Heart (22 HP) is breakable inside 3 turns.

## E. Can a team build and test this without guessing?

Mostly yes for rules (acceptance rows have exact numbers; `combatWith` extension is specified). The pending tests are `it.todo` strings with no assertions, so "ports exist" overstates readiness; at least the U rows (EM2, EM3, EA1 to EA10, WP1, CL1 to CL6, SV3 to SV5) could be real failing tests now. Guesses a team would still have to make: every item in D 4 and 5, mid-turn phase changes (B), who earns achievements in the career sim, and the sim's route and fuse policy.

**The curve depends on things the sim does not model.** BV2 (median first win 8 to 12) uses "the sensible path", defined in v1 as Brass upgrades only. In v2, power also comes from achievement-gated Masterworks and Rares, five residents (an extra trader, a pre-upgraded part, full layout reveal, oil), landmarks and the Clockmaker's memory. State the career bot's policy for them (which achievements it earns, when, and with what deterministic rule), or the target is untestable. Note also that 10 of the 17 medium and 4 of the 6 hard achievements require wins, with `m-all-chassis` needing three. At first win around run 10, a Legendary (needing a Master or Clockwork win and, for the Queen's core, the right run) arrives only after many dozens of runs for the owner. All 13 Rares start locked and all 10 Masterworks too; a new player sees only Commons and Uncommons for a long time. The owner asked for more tiers; easy achievements currently reward only cosmetics and journal pages. Put at least a Rare and one Masterwork behind early, non-win feats.

Also: the target for the expert's median first win moves from 2 (spike) to 8 to 12, and no-meta win rate from 36% to under 5%, a roughly 7x cut. That is retuning enemy HP and damage across the board, which also changes the human experience. The greedy bot (casual proxy) has no upper bound: add "greedy median first win at most 30" so the retune cannot lock casual players out.

## F. Scope

Realistic as a design, large as a build (v1 took six build phases):
- 70 parts, 36 trinkets, 24 enemy frames with about 80 distinct enemy parts, each painted and anchored with idle, attack and hurt (wardens also phase), broken-part visuals (WP7 requires a part to "stay shown broken"), 28 events, 30 achievements, 4 modes, 10 Overwind levels, 5 residents, 3 landmarks, a fourth chassis, a walkable Bellfoot, three painted cut-away act sections with generated room graphs laid over them (not specified how a painted background fits a seeded graph), walking animation between rooms, 9 ambient beds.
- Ship order I would hold to: (1) enemy machines, target order and salvage on the existing run loop; (2) the roam map with clock, elites, workbench and traders; (3) wardens with phases; (4) rarity, achievements, modes; (5) Bellfoot, residents, landmarks, memory; (6) Overwind and Legendaries.
- Cut or defer candidates: Overwind levels 6 to 10 (and the h-ow8 / h-ow10 chain), the Scrapper chassis, two of the five residents, landmarks beyond the lift, the walk animation between rooms (move on tap; keep Sprocket's walk in Bellfoot only), and Legendary trinkets if added. Keep: the title screen, Bellfoot as a menu-first street, the Clockmaker's memory (best "Spire remembers" piece, and cheap).

## Improvements (ranked, at most 3)

1. **Prove the Plating answer on paper before build.** Add a `Plating-bypass share` metric (enemy damage per fight that Plating cannot stop: Pierce, Corrode-removed, Siphon heal) with a per-act floor (suggest at least 25% of enemy damage potential on normals and 40% on elites and wardens), and rework act 1 and act 3 content to hit it: give act 1 regulars a real Pierce or Corrode (Corrode scaled to the stack, such as 10 plus half your Plating), give Orrery, Minute Warden and Clockmaker parts that strip or Pierce. Re-run the spike's turtle bot on the v2 numbers (even a scripted estimate) before D3 closes. Also make the core race cost something (for example regulars' parts attack for their cadence turn even if the core dies on the player's turn; or add a Hurry rule: wrecked parts drop 0 and Scrap halves) so "break the right part" is forced rather than hoped.
2. **Fix the targets so they can be satisfied and measure what the design actually does.** Replace BV3's win-rate clause with: expert HP lost at most 70% of the turtle's and the burst's on the same bins; add "Plating-covered turns (every shown attack absorbed) at most 60% of expert fights per act" and the bypass share from improvement 1; make the 2-turns-per-phase floor a rule with a unit test (WP8) and add a max-burst or scripted 100-per-turn player to BV4; add a greedy-career upper bound. Resolve the mid-turn phase change and cadence rules and update the data model's cadence, multi-action and escalate shapes in the same pass.
3. **Close the spec gaps against the playtest and the economy of the clock.** Add Masterwork and Legendary trinkets or log the exclusion; put a Rare and a Masterwork behind early non-win achievements; fix the Legendary source and Overwind-unlock contradictions; give the Spire clock a loss state (and decide the bell's value), validate hours against shortest-path length in CL1, and add a rusher and grinder route bot so BV2 cannot be gamed by either.
